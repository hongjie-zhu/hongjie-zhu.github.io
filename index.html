Biography

I am an undergraduate student in Biomedical Engineering in the joint programme between Northeastern University and the University of Dundee.

My work focuses on medical AI, including dynamic ultrasound interpretation, multimodal clinical decision support, and image-guided robotics. I am also interested in using AI to connect brain imaging with tissue-scale biological information.

Looking ahead, I hope to explore connections between molecular and cellular states, tissue structure, and medical imaging, while developing a broader understanding of biological proteins and molecular engineering.

Research

Medical AI connects my current work in ultrasound, robotics, and brain imaging. Protein and molecular engineering is a longer-term direction I hope to explore.

Ultrasound medical models

Learning from dynamic ultrasound and clinical context to support interpretation and intervention.

Dynamic TPUS and Green classification

September 2025 – present · Supervisor: Prof. Hong Li

I developed a workflow combining key-anatomy localization and attention-based temporal modeling for Green classification of cystocele across the Valsalva sequence.

The study included 881 patients, with 193 held out for prospective testing. Green-AttGRU achieved a macro-AUC of 0.939 and overall accuracy of 90.2%. In a nested reader study, pooled median interpretation time decreased from 26.7 to 9.9 seconds with AI assistance.

Paper

Real-time ultrasound guidance

March 2024 – present · Supervisor: Prof. Hong Li

I work on jointly localizing deforming lesions and fast-moving instrument tips during ultrasound-guided breast intervention. Detector-VABTR combines a lightweight MobileNetV4 backbone with dynamic receptive-field attention and multi-scale feature fusion.

The dataset contains 167 patients and 10,668 annotated keyframes. The held-out test results report lesion / instrument-tip mAP50 of 0.861 / 0.796. Clinical usability was evaluated in an offline playback crossover reader study.

Related publications

LRM-Fusion: imaging and clinical evidence

September 2024 – present · Supervisor: Prof. Hong Li

I designed an attention-based temporal imaging branch and a clinical-information fusion strategy to distinguish hepatocellular carcinoma from non-HCC malignancies in malignant CEUS LI-RADS LR-M lesions.

The revised manuscript reports an AUC of 0.898 and accuracy of 88.9% in a 99-patient multicenter test cohort. Evaluation includes calibration, decision curves, model interpretability, and a four-radiologist reader study.

Multimodal pregnancy risk modeling

July 2025 – present · Supervisor: Prof. Hong Li

I contributed to a non-invasive pregnancy-outcome prediction framework integrating transvaginal ultrasound, Doppler perfusion, short cine dynamics, clinical variables, and embryo-development stage.

My work includes gated multimodal fusion and evaluation with temporal validation, calibration, decision curves, and SHAP analysis. The manuscript is under review.

Medical robotics

Moving from visual perception to controlled physical interaction.

RCM-constrained bimanual laparoscopic robotics

June 2026 – present · Supervisor: Prof. Fangxun Zhong

I built a ROS 2 control architecture for a CR5 six-DoF arm and a self-built MPK five-axis mechanism. Validated components have been transferred to the physical platform for joint-space tracking and continuous motion.

A monocular vision pipeline supports ring localization, grasping, and transfer. Ongoing work extends the platform toward bimanual coordination and remote-center-of-motion (RCM) constraints. Simulation work uses RViz2, Gazebo Sim, trajectory control, and RGB-D sensing.

Robotic perception and geometric localization

September 2023 – September 2024 · Supervisor: Prof. Zhiguo Lu

As a team member, I implemented image acquisition, camera calibration, target detection, and 3D pose estimation. I used SolvePnP and Kalman filtering to improve localization robustness under image noise and target motion.

Brain science

Linking macroscopic brain images to interpretable tissue-scale representations.

TriSCoV-Net: virtual immunomarker proxies

May 2025 – present · Supervisor: Prof. Chao Li

I proposed a three-stage framework combining dMRI-conditioned synthesis, PLI-guided super-resolution, and cross-scale verification. A shared dMRI encoder, gated FiLM conditioning, and support-domain constraints address weak registration and scale mismatch.

Evaluation uses the Oxford Digital Brain Bank, subject-level leave-one-subject-out testing, and standardized ImageJ DAB quantification. The primary outputs are tissue-scale proxies for MRI–histology association, not replacements for physical IHC staining or single-cell measurements.

Biological proteins

Future research interest

I am interested in how protein engineering, ion-channel mechanisms, and molecular probes could help connect molecular or cellular states with medical imaging. This is a direction I hope to develop alongside my current AI and imaging work.

A longer-term question is how to bridge molecular signals, cellular function, tissue structure, and whole-organ imaging with measurable, cross-scale representations.
